Moves

    Rules
    • On your turn you may move a normal piece straight away by regular chess rules, or roll the D6 instead.
    • If you roll 1 to 5 you move a normal piece. If you roll a 6 you choose: move a Mega Knight, Giga Knight or a normal piece. If you can do neither, your turn is lost.
    • A Mega Knight starts in the center. It is red, neutral, and fills a 2x2 block. It leaps like a knight: moving 2 vertices in one direction, and 1 vortex left or right.
    • It crushes every enemy piece on the 4 squares it lands on, kings included. Crushing a king wins at once.
    • It cannot land on your own pieces. It can capture another Mega Knight only by landing exactly on top of it (a full 2x2 overlap); a partial overlap is not allowed. Normal pieces cannot capture it or pass through it.
    • Four Knights of any colors in a 2x2 block merge into a new Mega Knight. There is no limit.
    • Four Mega Knights in a 4x4 block merge into a light blue Giga Knight, a 3x3 piece. It moves like a shorter knight: 1 square away from it's middle (main) piece, and 1 square left or right, so it can overlap its own old position.
    • Online: tap Play online, create a private match and send the code (or invite link) to your opponent. The creator plays White first; sides swap each new game.
    • Check applies to normal pieces. If you roll 1 to 5, are in check and have no legal move, you are checkmated.

    Privacy policy

    Mega Knight Chess does not operate its own analytics or advertising system. However, the website's hosting provider and third-party services used for fonts and online multiplayer may process technical information, including IP addresses. This policy explains what happens.

    What the game collects

    Nothing. There are no accounts, names, emails, analytics, advertising, cookies or trackers, and the game's creator runs no server and receives no data from you. Playing on one device never sends anything anywhere.

    What is saved on your device

    The game stores a random reconnection token in your browser's local storage. Online play also uses a match code to help establish the connection between players. The match code and connection information may be processed by the multiplayer services described below.

    Online matches

    Online play connects the two devices directly (peer to peer). Only dice rolls and moves are sent, with no names, chat or other personal details. To set up the connection, third-party services see your IP address and the match's random code:

    • PeerJS signaling server (0.peerjs.com) helps the two devices find each other.
    • STUN servers (Google and Cloudflare) tell your device its public address.
    • TURN relay (openrelay.metered.ca, by Metered) passes your encrypted game traffic along only when no direct route exists.

    Your opponent's device also receives your IP address, as with any direct connection. Only share a match code with someone you trust, because anyone who has the code can join.

    Other things loaded from the internet

    • Google Fonts loads the game's fonts when the page opens, so Google receives your IP address and browser details.
    • PeerJS library is fetched from a public CDN (jsDelivr, unpkg or cdnjs) the first time you play online.
    • If you open the game from a website such as GitHub Pages, that host may keep ordinary access logs.

    These services have their own privacy policies, which the game does not control.

    Children and changes

    The game does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. If this policy changes, the new version will appear in this menu.
